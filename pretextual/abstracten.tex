\begin{abstracten}
\textbf{Context}: Design Decisions play a critical role in software architecture and meeting functional and quality requirements, but they can be challenging due to uncertainty and implicit knowledge. AI tools, such as Large Language Models (LLM) and Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPT), offer a promising solution for automated decision support. This research explores the characteristics and types of AI-assisted tools, their strengths and limitations, and their integration into various Software Development Life Cycle phases. \textbf{Objective:} This study aims to evaluate the potential of using AI-assisted tools to make design decisions in software projects. This will help us understand their strengths and limitations to guide their application in this context. \textbf{Methodology:} consists of a literature review, preliminary study, and multiple case studies. The literature review provided valuable information about the various approaches to design pattern selection. It revealed a gap in the absence of generative AIs for discovering such design patterns. The preliminary study focuses on using pre-trained AI tools to select design patterns in a specific context automatically. The accuracy of AI-generated responses and their potential to guide developers effectively are assessed. The subsequent case studies involve selecting at least three companies to participate. Design decisions related to architecture or software are identified for each company, and questions are formulated to support software architects. AI tools' responses are evaluated using prompt engineering techniques, and proposed solutions are presented in interview form to the architects for feedback. The data is analyzed using qualitative techniques such as Open Code to gain insights into the usefulness and applicability of AI tools in architectural and software design decision-making. \textbf{Outcomes:} This preliminary study explores the effectiveness of an AI-assisted tool in assisting developers in choosing a design pattern to solve design scenarios. To reach this goal, we gathered 56 existing questions used by teachers and public tenders that provide a concrete context and ask which design pattern would be suitable. We submitted these questions to ChatGPT and analyzed the answers. We found that 93\% of the questions were answered correctly with a good level of detail, demonstrating the potential of such a tool as a valuable resource to help developers apply design patterns and make design decisions.
\end{abstracten}
