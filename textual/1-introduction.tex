\section{Motivation}\label{sec:motivation}
% The decision-making process in software design is pivotal in establishing the architecture's structure, organization, and component linkages. Accurate and skilled decisions ensure the fulfillment of functional and quality requirements within the problem domain and solution space, influencing costs, deadlines, and the overall project outcome. Thus, considering cost, time, and team size is essential for the project's success. It is also vital to consider the perspectives of key stakeholders such as customers and investing companies, the project context, available development tools, software product methods, and prevailing uncertainties.

% Early in the software development lifecycle, before coding begins, engineers and architects must make nuanced trade-offs anticipating future scenarios. This stage, fraught with uncertainties and tacit knowledge, is critical yet prone to errors. Despite the advantages of Microservices and Event-Driven architectural styles in enhancing enterprise agility, they complicate decision-making due to their granular components and tight couplings, increasing the risk of errors and architectural debt.

The choices made in software design play a crucial role in shaping the architecture and establishing its structure, organization, and component connections~\cite{perry1992}. When made with care and skill, these decisions ensure accurate fulfillment of functional and quality requirements while considering the underlying demands of the problem domain and solution space~\cite{bhukya2016,yu2018,ahuja2019}. These decisions affect expenses, deadlines, and the project's overall outcome, making software successful or unsuccessful~\cite{garousi2019}.

Therefore, it is essential to consider factors such as cost, time, and size of the development team for overall project success. Key stakeholders, such as customers using the software and the company investing in the product, should also consider their perspectives. The decision-making process must also consider the project context, available development tools, software product methods, and conditions of uncertainty~\cite{berntsson2006,hicdurmaz2012,viviani2023empirical}.

Software design decision-making is typically made early in the software development lifecycle before any code is written~\cite{Farshidi2020}, often requiring engineers and software architects to make trade-offs when reasoning about future outcomes~\cite{Becker2017}. However, this process can be challenging and error-prone due to uncertainty, ambiguity, and tacit knowledge~\cite{Liang2023}.

Despite the advantages offered by Microservices~\cite{Boner2017, Newman2019} and Event-Driven~\cite{Bellemare2020} architectural styles, which have become prevalent in enterprises, managing design decisions has become more complex. The increased granularity of systemic components in microservices and the tight coupling in these architectures make it difficult to understand the overall system view. Consequently, the risk of errors and the accumulation of Architectural Technical Debt has increased~\cite{Ernst2015}.

Design patterns~\cite{Gamma1994, Martin2002, Joshua2004} should be strongly adopted and widely used in software development to mitigate the factors of lousy design decision-making and decrease technical debts. These benefits include code reuse, better organization, ease of maintenance, scalability, and software quality~\cite{Holub2004, Barros2018}. Implementing these patterns is essential for effective design decision-making, as they provide recurring solutions to common problems in specific contexts~\cite{buschmann1996pattern}. Design patterns also facilitate team communication, allowing code adaptability to accommodate changes. Therefore, incorporating Design Patterns into the software development process can significantly assist in making informed and efficient architectural decisions.

Mastering the understanding and implementation of design patterns presents a formidable challenge, as it requires a robust knowledge of abstract thinking and object-oriented programming~\cite{chang2011pattern}. Educational institutions have sought to help students overcome these obstacles by creating methods focused on improving recognition of design patterns and practical application~\cite{fojtik2014, lotz2014}. However, the voluminous variety and diversity of available patterns such as \href{https://microservices.io/patterns/microservices.html}{Microservices Patterns} and \href{https://refactoring.guru/design-patterns/catalog}{Design Patterns} and further complicate this effort, making this educational process arduous. 

Implementing AI-based solutions has been a long-standing plan to simplify identifying and applying design patterns. However, it poses several challenges as it demands vast amounts of data and rigorous training to teach AI how to interpret design patterns~\cite{Naghdipour2023}. This requirement often hinders companies' adoption, requiring significant time, resources, and specialized knowledge investments.

Applying Artificial Intelligence solutions to this problem has been part of the plan for some time. This AI tool aims to simplify the detection and application of design patterns by determining patterns given a context. However, it presents challenges as it requires enormous data and training to create models to teach AI how to interpret design patterns.

Generative Artificial Intelligence, specifically \ac{GPT} models, has opened new possibilities for individuals who lack specialized training. However, whether these generalized tools can efficiently identify and apply design patterns in real-world situations is still being determined. 

Our investigation aims to determine whether \ac{GPT} model-based tools can simplify the process of understanding and implementing design patterns. The initial study has shown promising results in selecting design patterns using these tools. 

Moreover, it is essential to understand how generative AI can assist architects in decision-making and how software architects perceive and utilize these tools.

\section{Context}\label{sec:context}
GPT models are artificial neural networks based on transformer architecture, pre-trained on vast datasets of unlabeled text. These models were introduced by OpenAI\footnote{\url{https://openai.com}} 2018. They can generate novel human-like content, making them suitable for various applications. ChatGPT\footnote{\url{https://chat.openai.com/}} is a generative AI Tool based on \ac{GPT} models that offer conversational interaction and problem-solving capabilities. The potential applications of ChatGPT have garnered interest from the media, the general public, and professional sectors such as public health~\cite{Biswas2023} and academia~\cite{Lund2023}. Recent research has explored the use of ChatGPT for software engineering tasks, including debugging~\cite{Surameery2023} and enhancing engineering requirements and code quality~\cite{White2023}.

The work of~\textcite{Ahmad2023towards} proposes DevBots, which are software development bots that~\ac{LLM} power. They enable collaboration between human architects and AI decision support in Architecture-Centric Software Engineering. The ChatGPT is used to refine artifact architectural designs through natural language processing. A case study demonstrates how ChatGPT collaborates with a novice software architect to design service-based software. Future research will aim to gather empirical evidence on the productivity of architects using ChatGPT and explore the sociotechnical aspects of AI architecture to effectively address Architecture-Centric Software Engineering challenges.

Integrating AI into design pattern selection has been another area of promising research.~\textcite{Naghdipour2023Semi} explored a semi-supervised learning approach based on text classification to evaluate design problems. The results indicated improved performance over traditional supervised learning techniques such as Naïve Bayes and K-Nearest Neighbor. 

Even with the increasing use of generative AI tools, humans play a significant role in decision-making, particularly with unstructured data and variables such as vision, strategy, and values. AI's integration can enhance decision-making, leading to more confident choices~\cite{Colson2019}.

This collaboration between generative AI tools and developers is relatively new, so it is crucial to understand its effectiveness and possible side effects, the nature of interactions, how these tools will be incorporated into the software development cycle, and the perceived benefits of particular interest. Furthermore, assessing whether using such tools leads to improvements or accelerates the software development process is necessary. Thus, detailed assessments of how developers and companies use generative AI tools in their software development cycles are essential to incorporate and optimize these new technologies successfully.

\section{Research Questions}\label{sec:research-questions}
\begin{quote}
\textbf{\textit{How do generative AI tools, specifically from GPT models, be used to support software architecture decision-making process?}}

\begin{itemize}
    \item  \textbf{\textit{RQ\textsubscript{1}: What is the efficiency and accuracy of generative AI tools, such as GPT, in solving software design problems?}}\newline To answer that question, we performed an exploratory study that used existing questions that asked the right design pattern for a design problem to investigate how generative AI tools perform.
    \item \textbf{\textit{RQ\textsubscript{2}: What approaches can be applied in using generative AI tools to support decision-making in software architecture?}}\newline To answer that question, our work is based on existing prompt patterns and uses design science to explore other alternatives. As a result, a new set of prompt patterns and a prompt sequence was proposed.
    \item \textbf{\textit{RQ\textsubscript{3}: What are the perceived benefits and challenges of using the proposed prompt sequence with generative AI tools in the architectural decision-making process from the perspective of software architects?}} \newline To answer that question, we applied the prompt sequence in past problems faced by companies and interviewed software architects that participated in the decision process to collect their impressions.   
\end{itemize}
\end{quote}

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}
The study's methodology is designed to assess the use of generative AI tools, specifically GPT models, to enhance decision-making processes in software architecture. The methodology consists of several phases, each contributing to evaluating generative AI tools' practical applicability and effectiveness in real-world scenarios. The research begins with an exploratory phase that examines the utility of generative AI tools in identifying and selecting design patterns. This phase involves a literature review to gather insights into existing design pattern selection methodologies and the integration of AI tools into these processes. A preliminary study using ChatGPT to assess its accuracy and efficiency in suggesting design patterns applicable to various software design challenges is conducted. This initial exploration is crucial for developing strategies to maximize the potential of AI tools in decision-making. Additionally, identifying prompt patterns that can guide the AI tool's response generation to the most relevant and contextually appropriate design patterns is a crucial aspect of directing AI capabilities in a way that aligns with specific architectural needs.

After gathering information in the initial phase, the research moves on to creating and refining prompt patterns. Five specific prompt patterns are developed to address common queries during software architectural decision-making. These patterns aim to capture the complexities of the design issues. It is essential to optimize the sequence in which these prompt patterns are presented to the AI tool. This optimization ensures that responses are accurate and logically organized, making understanding the AI's guidance easier.

The final phase evaluates the effectiveness of the sequence of prompt patterns developed through two industry studies in different sectors (financial institutions and pharmaceuticals) to assess this approach's practical benefits and implementation in real-world contexts. These studies are essential in validating theoretical models against real industry challenges and scenarios. Iterative validation of research steps against designed research questions anchors each phase within the study objectives, ensuring that the methodology remains focused and aligned with the overall research objectives.

\section{Originality}\label{sec:originality}
Five new architectural prompt patterns, sensitive to context, were developed and linked to a sequence of prompt patterns. Each step in this sequence is an architectural situation that addresses a given problem and assists architects with information for their architectural decisions. This approach can expand the usefulness of generative AI beyond its conventional applications.

An existing gap in work involving generative AI in architectural decision-making is the participation of the Architect in research. This research conducted industry studies that collect feedback from software architects, providing a practical assessment of the effectiveness and applicability of generative AI decision-making that enriches academic discourse with insights from real-world applications and identifies opportunities to refine AI tools to better meet the differentiated needs of software architecture.

\section{Relevance}\label{sec:relevance}
This master's work introduces an innovative approach to integrating generative artificial intelligence (AI), specifically GPT models, into software architectural decision-making. Through developing and applying a sequence of prompt patterns, the study proposes a structured method to guide AI tools to offer design suggestions, aiming to improve quality and efficiency in software development.

The relevance of this work stands out for its academic and practical contribution, testing and validating the approach in real scenarios and offering valuable insights into the application, effectiveness, and limitations of generative AI tools in software architecture.

Finally, facilitating the learning and adopting software design patterns is a vital advantage of this work. By simplifying the selection and application of these patterns, the approach can be a valuable educational tool for budding software architects and help development teams incorporate design best practices more effectively and efficiently. This aspect promotes continued education within the software development community and encourages the adoption of innovative practices that can lead to higher-quality software products.
